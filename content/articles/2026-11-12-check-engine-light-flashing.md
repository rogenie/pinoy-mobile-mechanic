A flashing check engine light means your car's computer has detected a serious problem that is happening right now, most often a severe engine misfire. It is more urgent than a solid light, because a misfire can overheat and ruin the catalytic converter, one of the most expensive parts on the car. Only a proper diagnosis finds what is actually causing it.

> This article explains what the problem means. It is general information, not repair instructions. A misfiring engine can damage the exhaust system, run unevenly and make parts under the hood dangerously hot. Leave testing and repairs to a licensed mechanic.

## What does a flashing check engine light mean?

A flashing check engine light is the car's way of saying "stop soon." A solid light means a fault has been stored and needs attention; a blinking light means the fault is active and causing damage as you drive.

The California Air Resources Board says a blinking light "indicates that a malfunction is occurring that could be damaging your catalytic converter" ([CARB](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/resources/fact-sheets/board-diagnostic-ii-obd-ii-systems-fact-sheet)). Mechanics answering drivers on RepairPal put it plainly: a flashing light "often indicates a serious issue most likely a misfire," and they advise not driving until it's resolved ([RepairPal](https://repairpal.com/my-check-engine-light-started-flashing-and-was-losing-power-or-misfiring)).

What it can feel like from the driver's seat: the engine shakes or stumbles at a stop, the car hesitates or feels weak when you press the gas, or you notice a fuel or burning smell. Some cars feel almost normal, which is why the light itself is the warning to take seriously.

## How common are check engine light problems?

Very common, and many drivers leave them alone. A check engine light is one of the most frequent warnings on the road, and surveys show a large share of owners wait months or never get it looked at.

| By the numbers | What the data shows |
| --- | --- |
| Drivers with the check engine light on | **22%** of drivers surveyed for CarMD's 2024 index, double the share in its previous survey ([NOLN](https://www.noln.net/site-placement/latest-news/news/55020696/study-nearly-half-of-drivers-ignoring-dashboard-warning-lights)) |
| Drivers with any warning light on | **49%** had some dashboard warning light lit ([NOLN](https://www.noln.net/site-placement/latest-news/news/55020696/study-nearly-half-of-drivers-ignoring-dashboard-warning-lights)) |
| Drivers who never get the light checked | **25%** leave it on indefinitely ([Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/survey-25-percent-of-you-ignore-your-check-engine-light)) |
| Drivers who wait a week to a year | **29.6%** ([Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/survey-25-percent-of-you-ignore-your-check-engine-light)) |
| Drivers who act within a week | Only **36.1%** ([Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-news/survey-25-percent-of-you-ignore-your-check-engine-light)) |
| Size of the repair data | CarMD's latest index analyzed more than **31 million** failures and recommended repairs ([CarPro](https://www.carpro.com/blog/carmd-top-10-reasons-for-a-check-engine-light-and-the-cost-to-repair)) |

Ignoring a solid light is a gamble. Ignoring a flashing one is how a few-hundred-dollar repair turns into a four-figure one.

## Is it safe to keep driving with a flashing check engine light?

No. A flashing check engine light is a sign to get off the road as soon as it is safe, stop driving, and call a professional. It is not a light to "keep an eye on" for the rest of the week.

The longer a severe misfire continues, the more damage it does. One guide warns that "every mile with a severe misfire sends raw fuel into the catalytic converter," and says to pull over if the car shakes heavily or you smell fuel or burning ([CarWhere](https://www.carwhere.com/warning-lights/flashing-check-engine-light)).

Timing makes it worse. The average San Diego County commute is about **25.5 minutes** each way ([FRED, U.S. Census data](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/B080ACS006073)), and many of those minutes are spent on I-15, I-805 and SR-52 at freeway speed. A light that starts blinking halfway to work leaves you choosing between an exit ramp and a ruined exhaust system. If the car is shaking or losing power in traffic, get yourself and your passengers to safety first.

## Why does a flashing check engine light matter more in San Diego?

Because California won't let you renew your registration with the light on. The Bureau of Automotive Repair states that "a vehicle will not pass a Smog Check with the check engine light on," and most cars need a Smog Check every other year to renew registration, plus before they are sold ([BAR](https://www.bar.ca.gov/smog-check)).

CARB adds that if a fault is keeping the light on, "you need to have the malfunctioning component repaired before you can pass the inspection" ([CARB](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/resources/fact-sheets/board-diagnostic-ii-obd-ii-systems-fact-sheet)). Computer-system failures are not rare: in the Bureau's 2021–2022 data, **7.0%** of model-year 2000 and newer cars tested at Smog Check failed the on-board diagnostic portion ([BAR Smog Check Performance Report](https://www.bar.ca.gov/pdf/smog-check-performance-report/2023.pdf)).

So for San Diego drivers, a flashing light that wrecks the catalytic converter is a double hit: an expensive repair, and a car you can't legally keep registered until it's done.

## Why is the cause so hard to pin down?

A flashing check engine light tells you something is wrong, not what. Several different problems can cause the same misfire and the same blinking light, and the stored fault code points to a symptom, not always the failed part.

That is where money disappears. Here is what the most common check engine repairs averaged in 2024, according to CarMD ([CarPro](https://www.carpro.com/blog/carmd-top-10-reasons-for-a-check-engine-light-and-the-cost-to-repair)):

- Ignition coils and spark plugs together: **$400**
- Ignition coils alone: **$218**
- Oxygen sensors: **$254**
- Fuel injectors: **$464**
- Catalytic converters: **$1,348**

Swap the wrong part and you pay for it, the misfire continues, and the catalytic converter keeps taking damage. CarMD notes that catalytic converters on newer cars usually don't fail unless a related root cause, such as a bad spark plug or oxygen sensor, is ignored ([CarPro](https://www.carpro.com/blog/carmd-top-10-reasons-for-a-check-engine-light-and-the-cost-to-repair)). Finding the real cause the first time is what protects the expensive part.

## What does it cost to put it off?

Putting off a flashing check engine light rarely saves money. The average check engine repair in 2024 was **$415.31** ([CarPro](https://www.carpro.com/blog/carmd-top-10-reasons-for-a-check-engine-light-and-the-cost-to-repair)), while the catalytic converter a misfire can destroy averaged more than three times that.
