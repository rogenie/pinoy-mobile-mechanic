When a car battery keeps dying overnight, something is either draining power while the car is parked, the battery itself is worn out, or the car isn't recharging it properly on the road. It's one of the most common reasons drivers get stranded, and because several different problems leave you with the same dead battery in the morning, only a proper diagnosis finds the real cause.

> This article explains what the problem means. It is general information, not repair instructions. A car's electrical system can cause burns, sparks, injury or expensive damage to the car's electronics if it's handled incorrectly. Leave testing and repairs to a licensed mechanic.

## How common is a car battery that keeps dying?

Very common. Dead batteries are the second biggest reason U.S. drivers call for roadside help, behind only tows.

| By the numbers | What the data shows |
| --- | --- |
| Roadside calls in one year | AAA answered **over 27 million** emergency roadside calls in 2024 ([Tow Times](https://towtimes.com/?p=85116)) |
| Battery calls in that year | About **7 million** of those calls were battery problems ([Tow Times](https://towtimes.com/?p=85116)) |
| Battery and tow calls combined | Roughly **74%** of all AAA roadside calls ([Tow Times](https://towtimes.com/?p=85116)) |
| Battery calls every summer | Nearly **2 million** battery-related calls each summer ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/car-batteries/how-hot-weather-affects-your-car-battery-what-to-do-about-it)) |
| Typical battery life | **Three to five years** under normal conditions ([AAA](https://southjersey.aaa.com/automotive/dont-fall-for-these-six-battery-drains)) |
| Drivers caught off guard | **Two thirds** of Americans had never had their battery tested before their car failed to start ([AAA](https://southjersey.aaa.com/news/automotive/roadside-breakdowns-preventable-proper-maintenance-finds-aaa)) |

A battery that dies once is annoying. A battery that keeps dying is a sign the real problem is still there.

## Why does the battery die overnight and not while driving?

A car battery dies overnight because that's when nothing is recharging it. While the engine runs, the charging system tops the battery up. Once the car is parked, every bit of power the car uses comes straight out of the battery until morning.

A modern car never fully switches off. Its computer modules stay awake for a while after you lock the doors, then go to sleep. According to [Vehicle Service Pros](https://vehicleservicepros.com/service-repair/battery-and-electrical/article/21281563/parasitic-battery-drain-is-a-bandit), a healthy car's draw should not exceed **50 milliamps** once the modules have gone to sleep, and all modules should be asleep within **75 minutes** of shutdown. A draw close to **1 amp or more** can drain a battery overnight.

Mechanics call this a parasitic draw. Common culprits that keep pulling power after the car is off include:

- Headlights left on, which can drain a fully charged battery in **one to two hours** ([AAA](https://southjersey.aaa.com/automotive/dont-fall-for-these-six-battery-drains))
- Interior, trunk or glove box lights that stay on because something wasn't fully closed
- Devices plugged into the 12-volt socket
- A key fob kept within **15 feet** of the car, which keeps the car's receiver awake ([AAA](https://southjersey.aaa.com/automotive/dont-fall-for-these-six-battery-drains))
- Aftermarket stereos and alarms, especially when wired incorrectly
- A computer module that won't go to sleep, a stuck relay, or damaged wiring

What it can feel like: the car is fine all day, then won't start the next morning. Or it starts sluggishly after a weekend parked. Or it dies again a week after a jump.

## Why is San Diego so hard on car batteries?

Heat, not cold, does the most damage to car batteries. High under-hood temperatures speed up the wear inside a battery, so it loses capacity faster and has less in reserve to survive a night parked.

The numbers:

- Car batteries last **58 months or longer** in the northernmost parts of the U.S., but **less than 41 months** in the southernmost regions ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/car-batteries/how-hot-weather-affects-your-car-battery-what-to-do-about-it)).
- Batteries in the southern U.S. last, on average, **17 months less** than in colder northern climates, and under-hood temperatures can exceed **150 degrees** in some parts of the country ([AAA](https://mwg.aaa.com/via/car/prevent-heat-damage-car-battery)).

The San Diego coast is mild, but inland neighborhoods like Escondido, Poway, Ramona, Santee and El Cajon get long runs of hot days. A battery that spent summer baking in a driveway there may still start the car, but with so little reserve that any small drain overnight is enough to leave it flat. Short trips make it worse: a quick drive to the store doesn't give the charging system much time to put back what the night took out.

## Why is it so hard to find what's draining the battery?

A battery that keeps dying is one of the hardest electrical problems to pin down. A worn-out battery, a faulty [alternator](/blog/alternator-replacement-cost/) that isn't charging, and a hidden drain all end with the same symptom: a car that won't start in the morning.

Even for professionals, drains take patience. According to [Vehicle Service Pros](https://vehicleservicepros.com/service-repair/battery-and-electrical/article/21281563/parasitic-battery-drain-is-a-bandit):

- Some drains are intermittent. They run long enough to kill the battery, then stop, so they're hard to catch in the act.
- A module can wake up overnight and wake other modules with it, without leaving any clue behind.
- Simply opening a door or touching a handle wakes the car's network and restarts the long wait before an accurate reading.
- Shortcuts lead to misdiagnosis.

That's where drivers lose money. A common pattern:

1. The car won't start, so the owner buys a new battery.
2. The car starts fine for a few days, then dies again, because the battery wasn't the cause.
3. The owner has now paid for a part the car didn't need, and still has the problem.

It's no wonder drivers are wary. In a national AAA survey, **two out of three** U.S. drivers said they don't trust auto repair shops in general. The top reasons were recommending unnecessary services (**76%**) and overcharging (**73%**) ([AAA](https://newsroom.aaa.com/2016/12/u-s-drivers-leery-auto-repair-shops/)). Replacing parts by guesswork is exactly how that happens.

## What does it cost to keep putting it off?

A battery that keeps dying doesn't fix itself. Each deep drain wears the battery further, so the problem tends to get worse and the car eventually fails somewhere you didn't plan for.

| --- | --- |
| New battery, professionally installed | **$268 to $368** ([Jerry](https://jerry.ai/car-repair/estimates/battery-replacement/)) |
| Local tow, first 5 to 10 miles | **$75 to $150** in 2026, then **$3 to $7** per mile ([TowRatings](https://towratings.net/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-tow-a-car)) |
| Tow at night, on weekends or holidays | **25% to 50%** surcharge at most companies ([TowRatings](https://towratings.net/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-tow-a-car)) |

| Getting around meanwhile | Rideshares, missed shifts, borrowed cars |

The worst case is paying for a new battery, a tow and a lost day, then having the same car die again next week because the drain was never found.

If the car dies somewhere unsafe, like a freeway shoulder on I-15, I-805 or SR-52, get yourself and your passengers to safety first, then call for help. If your car only clicks when you turn the key, see [what a clicking no-start means](/blog/car-wont-start-clicking-noise/).
