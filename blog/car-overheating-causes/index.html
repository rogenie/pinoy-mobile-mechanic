Car Overheating? Common Causes and What to Do on the Freeway

November 24, 2026 · Pinoy Mobile Mechanic, San Diego

A car overheating means the engine is making more heat than its cooling system can carry away. It is one of the most common reasons cars end up on a tow truck, and it can come from a long list of parts, from low coolant to a failing water pump or a blown head gasket. The temperature gauge can't tell you which one, so only a proper diagnosis finds the cause.

This article explains what the problem means. It is general information, not repair instructions. Coolant in an overheated engine is under pressure and hot enough to cause serious burns. Leave testing and repairs to a licensed mechanic.

How common is car overheating?

Engine cooling failures are one of the top three reasons cars break down badly enough to need a tow. AAA ranks them alongside battery problems and tire damage, and older cars are hit hardest.

By the numbers What the data shows
Normal engine temperature About 195 to 220°F when everything works (AAA)
AAA roadside calls in one year More than 32 million U.S. requests in 2017 (AAA)
Cooling failures among serious breakdowns 11% of breakdowns serious enough for a tow were engine cooling failures (AAA)
Older cars and tows Cars 10 years and older are 4 times as likely to need a tow (AAA)
How old the fleet is More than half of cars on the road are over 10 years old (AAA)
Water pump life Most water pumps last about 60,000 to 100,000 miles (AAA)

In plain terms: if your car is past 10 years old, a cooling problem is a matter of when, not if. AAA notes cooling failures are particularly common in vehicles 10 years and older (AAA).

What does an overheating car look and feel like?

An overheating car usually shows a temperature gauge climbing toward red or a warning light, and often steam, a sweet or burning smell, or a sudden loss of power. These are signs that something is wrong, not a way to tell which part failed.

Warning signs AAA lists include (AAA):

  • A temperature gauge or light in the red
  • A burning smell
  • Thumping or ticking sounds from the engine
  • Coolant under the car
  • Steam from under the hood
  • Decreased engine power
  • An extremely hot hood

Coolant pooling under a parked car may point to a leak that could lead to overheating (Cars.com). Two cars with the same steam and the same gauge reading can have completely different problems.

What should you do if your car overheats on the freeway?

If your car overheats on the freeway, get to the shoulder or an exit as soon as it is safe, turn the engine off and call for help. AAA says that when the gauge reads red, you should pull over and turn the engine off immediately (AAA).

Why it matters so much:

  • Damage starts fast. Firestone notes damage can begin within just a minute or two of overheating, and that there is no safe amount of time to keep driving an overheating engine (Firestone).
  • The coolant is dangerous. Coolant in a car that has overheated may be in excess of 230 degrees (GEICO). AAA Club Alliance warns the liquid is under pressure and could be boiling hot, and says never to remove a radiator cap until everything has cooled, usually 40 to 60 minutes (AAA Club Alliance).
  • Cooling takes time. It will take at least 15 minutes for the engine to cool (GEICO), and AAA warns that adding coolant yourself and driving on is dangerous and can cause severe burns (AAA).

Stay away from the hood, stay safely off the traffic lanes and let a professional take it from there. On a busy stretch of I-15 or I-805, your safety comes before the car.

Why does San Diego make overheating worse?

San Diego makes overheating more likely because inland heat waves and stop-and-go commutes both push cooling systems to their limit. AAA says summer heat puts extra strain on the radiator, thermostat, water pump and fan belts (AAA).

  • Inland heat. In this September's heat wave, Escondido reached 112 degrees, and Kearny Mesa set a record high of 103. El Cajon set a record warm low of 77 degrees, so engines never got a cool night (ABC 10News).
  • Traffic. The average San Diego car commuter now spends a record 88 hours a year stuck in traffic, up 24 hours since 2019 (Axios San Diego). Crawling on SR-52 or I-15 in the heat means less air moving through the radiator.
  • Hills. Climbs like the grade up to Ramona or Mount Helix make the engine work hard right when the air is hottest.

A cooling system that was barely coping in spring can give out on the first hot afternoon in Santee or Escondido.

Why is the cause of overheating so hard to pin down?

The cause of overheating is hard to pin down because at least eight different problems produce the same rising gauge. AAA lists coolant issues, thermostat failure, a damaged radiator, a faulty serpentine belt, a bad water pump, clogged or leaking coolant hoses, a blown head gasket and low oil (AAA).

That overlap is where drivers lose money. A common pattern:

  1. The car runs hot, so the owner adds coolant and keeps driving.
  2. It overheats again, so a thermostat gets replaced on a guess.
  3. The real problem was a leaking water pump, and by now the engine has been cooked several times.

Each round of guessing costs money and puts more heat through the engine. For a sense of how one unclear symptom can hide several causes, see our guide to a flashing check engine light.

What does it cost to put off an overheating problem?

Putting off an overheating problem can turn a few-hundred-dollar repair into engine damage. GEICO puts it bluntly: what you do can mean the difference between a $20 thermostat and thousands of dollars in repairs (GEICO). Driving on can warp cylinder heads or crack the engine block (Firestone).

Frequently asked questions

What does it mean when my car is overheating?

It means the engine is running hotter than the cooling system can handle. A normal engine runs at about 195 to 220 degrees Fahrenheit, and causes range from low coolant or a failing thermostat to a bad water pump, radiator or head gasket. Only a proper diagnosis finds which one.

Can I keep driving if my car is overheating?

No. Pull over as soon as it is safe, turn the engine off and call a professional. Firestone notes engine damage can begin within a minute or two of overheating.

How much does it cost to fix an overheating car?

It depends on the cause. Jerry puts the range at less than $100 to over $1,000, with water pumps typically $400 to $800, and RepairPal estimates radiator replacement at $1,368 to $1,550. Driving on until the engine is damaged costs far more.

Do I need a tow if my car overheated?

Not always. If the car is parked safely, a mobile mechanic can come to it and find the cause there. Driving an overheating car to a shop risks turning a small repair into engine damage.
